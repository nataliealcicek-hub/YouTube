# Viral Script Pack — 5 Kurzgesagt-Style Scripts

Built directly from the archetype analysis in `kurzgesagt-viral-video-analysis.md`. Each script targets one proven viral archetype, follows the channel's 6-beat skeleton (hook → reframe → build → turn → meaning → outro), and stacks the "common DNA" traits: second-person present tense, scale whiplash, one high-arousal emotion, concrete metaphors, and an optimistic landing.

**Format key:** `[V]` = visual direction · plain text = narration VO · `(M)` = music cue.
**Target runtime per script:** ~6–8 min (~1,100–1,300 words spoken).

# SCRIPT 1 — "What If You Fell Into Jupiter?"
**Archetype A — Cosmic "What If You…"** · Emotion: wonder + dread
**Alt titles:** "Falling Into Jupiter Is Worse Than You Think" · "Jupiter Would Kill You 7 Times"
**Thumbnail:** tiny bird in a spacesuit falling toward a huge striped Jupiter, flat dark-blue background.

### HOOK (0:00–0:40)
`[V: A serene shot of Jupiter from space. A tiny astronaut-bird floats into frame, waves.]`
Jupiter is beautiful. A giant marble of storms, a thousand times the volume of Earth. From up here, it almost looks soft. Like you could dive in.

`[V: The bird leans forward. Beat. It starts falling.]`
So let's dive in. Today, you're going to fall into Jupiter — and you're going to die. Several times, actually. Let's count them.

`(M: playful-ominous theme kicks in.)` `[V: TITLE CARD.]`

### BEAT 1 — THE LIE OF THE SURFACE (0:40–2:00)
`[V: Zoom toward the cloud tops; a "SURFACE?" sign gets stamped, then crossed out.]`
First, a problem: Jupiter has no surface. There is nothing to land on. It's a planet made almost entirely of atmosphere — hydrogen and helium, wrapped around a mystery. Falling into Jupiter isn't hitting a floor. It's sinking into a bottomless sky.

At the cloud tops, things are almost pleasant. The gravity is only about two and a half times Earth's. You'd weigh as much as a fridge, but you'd survive. Enjoy this part. It's the last good part.

### BEAT 2 — THE DESCENT / ESCALATION (2:00–4:30)
`[V: Depth meter on screen ticking down; the world darkens in stages.]`
Fifty kilometers down: you fall through clouds of ammonia ice at minus 145 degrees Celsius. **Death number one: freezing.** But let's say your suit holds.

A hundred kilometers down: the pressure doubles, then doubles again. Winds here move at hundreds of kilometers per hour — storms wider than Earth that have raged for centuries. **Death number two: shredded.** But let's say you slip between the storms.

`[V: The light dies completely. Only the suit lamp. It gets warm, then orange.]`
A thousand kilometers down: total darkness, and the temperature starts climbing. The pressure is now like being under a kilometer of ocean — except the ocean is sky, and it never ends. **Death number three: crushed.** Your bones give up here. Jupiter does not notice.

### BEAT 3 — THE TURN: THE IMPOSSIBLE OCEAN (4:30–6:00)
`(M: shift — awe, strings, something almost sacred.)`
`[V: The atmosphere thickens until gas becomes liquid — a glowing, silver, endless sea.]`
But here's the part almost nobody knows. Keep falling — past every death — and the sky around you stops being sky. The pressure becomes so extreme that hydrogen gas is squeezed into something that shouldn't exist: a shimmering ocean of *liquid metallic hydrogen*, tens of thousands of kilometers deep.

It conducts electricity. It generates the largest magnetic field of any planet. You are now falling through the dynamo of a world — an ocean no eye will ever see, glowing under pressures that turn physics strange. Somewhere below you may be a core of rock and exotic matter, hotter than the surface of the sun.

You don't reach it. Nothing ever has. Even our best probe, plunging in 1995, survived less than an hour before Jupiter erased it.

### BEAT 4 — MEANING / PAYOFF (6:00–7:00)
`[V: Pull back out — the whole planet again, serene. The little bird reappears safely on a spaceship, shaken, holding cocoa.]`
So why imagine any of this? Because this is what most of the universe is like. Gentle worlds with surfaces, sunlight, and air — worlds like ours — are the rare exception. Almost everything out there would kill you instantly, beautifully, in ways your body can't even register.

And yet — you got to know this. A soft, fragile creature on a small blue exception of a planet just fell through the heart of a gas giant, in its mind, for free. The universe is mostly deadly. But you're the part of it that gets to *understand* that. That's not a small thing.

### OUTRO (7:00–7:30)
`[V: End screen, two video cards, bird waves.]`
If you enjoyed dying seven times today, you might enjoy our other videos on the strangest places in the solar system. Sources in the description. See you in the next one.

# SCRIPT 2 — "You Might Be the Last Generation to Die"
**Archetype B — Existential Mystery / Provocation** · Emotion: unsettled hope
**Alt titles:** "What If Death Becomes Optional?" · "The Last Mortal Generation"
**Thumbnail:** a bird looking at an hourglass where the sand flows *upward*, flat warm background.

### HOOK (0:00–0:45)
`[V: A line of birds of increasing age — child, adult, elderly — walking toward a door marked with a tombstone. The line suddenly stops. The last bird looks back at the camera.]`
Every human who has ever lived has died, or is going to. A hundred billion people so far. It is the one rule with no exceptions.

But you happen to be alive at a very strange moment. Because for the first time in history, serious scientists are asking — not *whether* we can slow aging — but *when.* And some think the answer is: soon. Maybe soon enough for you.

Which raises the strangest question a generation has ever faced: what if you're among the last people who have to die — or the first who don't?

`(M: pensive main theme.)` `[V: TITLE CARD.]`

### BEAT 1 — REFRAME: AGING IS NOT A LAW (0:45–2:30)
`[V: A cell as a small machine, slowly rusting; repair-bots buffing it back to new.]`
Here's the reframe. For most of history we treated aging like gravity — a fixed law of the universe. But biologically, it isn't a law. It's a process. Damage builds up in your cells faster than your body repairs it, and over decades, the machinery wears down. And a process can, in principle, be slowed, paused, or reversed.

`[V: Naked mole-rats, jellyfish, and ancient trees parade past, unbothered by time.]`
Nature already proves it. Some jellyfish can revert to a younger state. Certain trees live for thousands of years. Naked mole-rats barely age at all. Aging is not required by life. It's just how *our* particular machine happens to run.

### BEAT 2 — THE ESCALATION: THE TOOLS ARE ARRIVING (2:30–4:15)
`[V: A lab bench; sliders labeled "cellular repair," "senescent cells," "reprogramming" being nudged upward.]`
And in the last decade, we've started building the tools to change it. Drugs that clear out worn-out "zombie" cells. Techniques that reprogram old cells to behave young again. In the lab, scientists have already extended the healthy lifespan of mice — meaningfully, repeatedly.

`(M: hope tightens into something more urgent.)`
None of this is a magic pill yet. It may not be for decades. It may not come in time. But the direction is no longer science fiction. For the first time, "curing aging" is a research goal with funding, results, and a roadmap.

### BEAT 3 — THE TURN: THE WEIGHT OF A LONG LIFE (4:15–5:45)
`(M: shift — the hopeful theme darkens, thoughtful.)`
`[V: A single bird given a very long road ahead; the road is beautiful, but it stretches past the horizon into fog.]`
But suppose it works. Suppose you could live for centuries. That's not a simple gift. Who gets it first — everyone, or only those who can afford it? What happens to a world where no one makes room for the next generation? What does a mind even *feel* like after three hundred years of memory?

We have spent our whole existence building meaning *around* death — our religions, our art, our urgency. Take death away, and we'd have to rebuild what a life is *for.* That's not a reason to fear it. But it's a reason to think, now, before the choice arrives.

### BEAT 4 — MEANING / PAYOFF (5:45–6:45)
`[V: Pull back — the line of aging birds from the opening, but now the road forward is unwritten, blank, waiting.]`
You may still die. Most likely, you will. This might all arrive a century too late for anyone watching. But you are alive at the exact moment humanity stopped treating death as inevitable and started treating it as a *problem* — and problems, we have a habit of solving.

Whether or not it happens in time for you, you get to witness the turning point. The first generation to look the oldest enemy in the eye and say: maybe not forever. That's a strange and remarkable place to be standing.

### OUTRO (6:45–7:15)
`[V: End screen, two video cards, bird waves.]`
Would you want to live for centuries? Tell us in the comments — we read them. Sources below. See you in the next one, hopefully for a very long time.

# SCRIPT 3 — "What If You Stopped Sleeping?"
**Archetype C — Your Body Is an Epic / Body Horror** · Emotion: creeping dread → relief
**Alt titles:** "Your Brain Is Cleaning Itself Right Now" · "Why You Can't Skip Sleep"
**Thumbnail:** a wide-eyed exhausted bird with a cracking brain, flat purple night background.

### HOOK (0:00–0:40)
`[V: A bird lying awake in bed, eyes open in the dark, clock glowing 3:47 AM.]`
You spend about a third of your entire life unconscious, paralyzed, and blind to the world. From an evolutionary standpoint, that's insane — it's dangerous, it wastes time, it leaves you defenseless. So sleep must be doing something so important that nature was willing to take that risk.

`[V: Push in through the bird's forehead into the brain.]`
So what happens if you don't? Let's find out — hour by hour — as you try to stay awake forever.

`(M: slow, ticking, uneasy.)` `[V: TITLE CARD.]`

### BEAT 1 — THE FIRST DAY (0:40–2:00)
`[V: A clock ticking; the brain-city dims one district at a time.]`
After 24 hours awake, your brain is already impaired to the level of being legally drunk. Reaction times slow. Your mood sours. Small tasks feel heavy. You're still functional — but your brain is starting to take involuntary "microsleeps," blackouts a fraction of a second long, where you're technically asleep with your eyes open and don't even know it.

### BEAT 2 — THE ESCALATION (2:00–4:00)
`[V: The brain-city's cleaning crews stand idle; garbage piles up in the streets between neurons.]`
Here's the part that should worry you. While you sleep, your brain does something it can't do awake: it cleans itself. Channels open up and flush out the metabolic garbage that builds up during the day — including the very proteins linked to Alzheimer's. Skip sleep, and the trash just… accumulates.

`[V: Days tick by. The bird hallucinates — furniture breathing, shadows moving.]`
By day three, people start to hallucinate. The line between dreaming and waking breaks down, because your brain is so desperate for sleep it starts forcing dream-states onto reality. Memory falters. Emotions swing wildly. Your immune system weakens. Your body is, quite literally, beginning to fall apart from the inside.

### BEAT 3 — THE TURN: THE LIMIT (4:00–5:30)
`(M: tension peaks, then an eerie calm.)`
`[V: The brain-city on emergency power, red lights, systems shutting down to protect the core.]`
You cannot win this fight. Eventually the pressure to sleep becomes so overwhelming that your brain simply overrides you and shuts down whether you like it or not. In extreme cases in animals, total sleep deprivation is fatal. There is a rare genetic disease that stops people from sleeping at all — and it is, without exception, deadly. Sleep is not optional. It is not laziness. It is maintenance your survival depends on.

### BEAT 4 — MEANING / PAYOFF (5:30–6:30)
`(M: warm resolve.)`
`[V: The bird finally sleeps; the cleaning crews sweep the city clean, lights softly glowing, memories filed neatly into shelves.]`
So tonight, when you lie down and lose consciousness, you're not wasting time. You're running the single most important maintenance cycle your body has. Memories are being sorted and saved. Your brain is being washed clean. Your body is repairing itself, cell by cell.

Sleep is the price of a functioning mind — and it might be the best deal your body ever made. So do yourself a favor. Get some tonight.

### OUTRO (6:30–7:00)
`[V: End screen, bird snoring in a tiny bed, two video cards float up.]`
If this made you sleepy, that's your brain being smart. Sources in the description. Goodnight — and see you in the next one.

# SCRIPT 4 — "What If the Sun Disappeared Right Now?"
**Archetype D — The Forbidden / Provocative "What If"** · Emotion: guilty curiosity → awe
**Alt titles:** "You Wouldn't Notice for 8 Minutes" · "The Day the Sun Vanished"
**Thumbnail:** a bird standing on a dark Earth where the sun is a black circle, flat deep-blue background.

### HOOK (0:00–0:40)
`[V: A sunny, ordinary afternoon. Birds picnicking. The sun shining.]`
Right now, the Sun is doing what it's done for four and a half billion years: holding your entire world together, and keeping you warm from 150 million kilometers away.

`[V: Snap. The sun blinks out. But everything on Earth stays sunny.]`
Now imagine it simply vanished. Blinked out of existence in an instant. Here's the eerie part — for the next eight minutes, you wouldn't notice a single thing. Let's live through the day the Sun disappeared.

`(M: ominous low drone.)` `[V: TITLE CARD.]`

### BEAT 1 — THE EIGHT-MINUTE LIE (0:40–2:00)
`[V: Sunlight still streaming; a countdown timer: 8:20 ticking down.]`
Light takes about eight minutes and twenty seconds to travel from the Sun to Earth. So the sunlight hitting your face right now actually left the Sun eight minutes ago. If the Sun vanished, the sky would stay bright, the day would look normal, and you'd go on with your afternoon — for eight more minutes — bathed in the light of a star that no longer exists.

`[V: Gravity waves too — the same delay. Earth still curving around an empty point.]`
And gravity would keep holding us in orbit for those same eight minutes. Earth would circle a Sun that isn't there anymore, blissfully unaware.

### BEAT 2 — THE MOMENT IT HITS (2:00–3:45)
`(M: the drop.)`
`[V: The timer hits zero. The sky goes black instantly. Earth lurches free of its orbit and flies off in a straight line.]`
Then, at eight minutes twenty, everything changes at once. The sky goes dark. And Earth, suddenly released, stops curving — and shoots off into space in a straight line, at 30 kilometers per second, like a stone flung from a sling. We would become a rogue planet, wandering the galaxy in total darkness.

`[V: The Moon and planets wink out one by one as the reflected sunlight runs out.]`
The Moon goes dark. The planets vanish from the night sky. All that light was borrowed from the Sun, and the loan just got called in.

### BEAT 3 — THE TURN: THE SLOW FREEZE (3:45–5:15)
`[V: A temperature gauge dropping; frost creeping across the world.]`
Now, the good news: you would not freeze instantly. Earth holds heat surprisingly well. Within a week, the average surface temperature would drop below zero. Within a year, it'd hit minus 100. The oceans would freeze from the top down — but here's the strange twist: that thick ice would actually *insulate* the water below, and the deep oceans would stay liquid, warmed by the Earth's own core, for thousands of years.

`(M: a flicker of unexpected hope.)`
`[V: Deep-sea vents glowing in the black ocean, tiny life clustered around them.]`
And around volcanic vents on the pitch-black sea floor, some life — the same kind that may have started it all — could hang on far longer than you'd ever guess. Life doesn't give up easily.

### BEAT 4 — MEANING / PAYOFF (5:15–6:15)
`(M: rises into awe.)`
`[V: Pull back to the warm, sunny picnic from the opening.]`
The Sun will not disappear. It's stable, and it has billions of years left. But playing the game shows you something we forget every single day: almost everything you love — warmth, light, the seasons, the very ground staying under your feet — is a free gift from a giant ball of fire you never think about.

`[V: The little bird looks up at the sun, shields its eyes, and smiles.]`
You are alive because a star is shining. That's worth looking up for, once in a while.

### OUTRO (6:15–6:45)
`[V: End screen, bird wearing tiny sunglasses, two video cards.]`
Go outside and catch some eight-minute-old sunlight while you can. Sources below. See you in the next one.

# SCRIPT 5 — "Why You Feel Lonely (Even When You're Not Alone)"
**Archetype F — Emotional / Philosophical Departure** · Emotion: recognition → comfort
**Alt titles:** "Loneliness Is a Signal, Not a Flaw" · "The Most Human Feeling"
**Thumbnail:** a single small bird glowing warm in a large cold-blue crowd, flat background.

### HOOK (0:00–0:50)
`[V: A bird in the middle of a busy, bustling crowd — everyone connected by glowing threads except this one, standing dim and alone.]`
You can be surrounded by people — at a party, in a city of millions, scrolling through hundreds of friends — and still feel completely, achingly alone. It's one of the strangest and most painful feelings a human can have. And almost everyone, at some point, feels it.

`[V: The lonely bird looks around, doesn't understand why.]`
But loneliness isn't a flaw in you, or a sign that something's broken. It's one of the oldest and most important signals your body has. Let me show you what it's really trying to tell you.

`(M: soft, tender piano.)` `[V: TITLE CARD.]`

### BEAT 1 — REFRAME: LONELINESS IS HUNGER (0:50–2:20)
`[V: A "fuel gauge" for connection sitting on empty, blinking red, next to a stomach growling.]`
Here's the reframe. Loneliness is not sadness. It's closer to *hunger.* For millions of years, our ancestors survived only in groups. Alone, a human was food. Together, we were unstoppable. So evolution wired a painful alarm into us — one that fires when we drift too far from other people, pushing us back toward the group that keeps us alive.

`[V: Ancient humans around a fire; a lone one on the dark edge feels the pull to return.]`
That ache you feel? It's that ancient alarm. It's not weakness. It's your body doing exactly what it was built to do: protecting you by reminding you that you need others.

### BEAT 2 — THE MODERN TRAP (2:20–4:00)
`(M: the theme cools, more complex.)`
`[V: The bird alone in a glowing apartment, phone in hand, surrounded by hundreds of tiny profile pictures that don't quite reach it.]`
But there's a cruel twist in the modern world. We've never been more *connected* — and yet loneliness is rising. We have hundreds of online friends and no one to call at 2 AM. We mistake being *around* people for being *known* by them. The alarm evolved for physical closeness, real faces, shared time — and a screen full of strangers can't quite switch it off.

`[V: The threads connecting people flicker — some real and warm, many thin and cold.]`
And loneliness lies to you. It whispers that you're unlikable, that reaching out is pointless, that everyone else is fine without you. Chronically lonely people start expecting rejection, so they pull away — which makes them lonelier. The alarm meant to pull you toward people can, left alone too long, quietly push you away.

### BEAT 3 — THE TURN: WHAT IT'S FOR (4:00–5:15)
`(M: warmth returns, resolve building.)`
`[V: The bird takes a small, brave step — sends one message, knocks on one door.]`
So here's the important part. Loneliness is not a verdict on your worth. It's a *signal* — like hunger, or thirst, or pain. And like those, it's asking you to do something. Not something huge. One text. One call. One small, genuine moment with one real person. Connection isn't built from hundreds of contacts. It's built from a few people who truly see you.

`[V: One glowing thread reconnects, warm and solid. The bird brightens.]`
And almost everyone around you feels this too — including the people you assume are perfectly fine. The person you're afraid to reach out to may be waiting, just as afraid, for you.

### BEAT 4 — MEANING / PAYOFF (5:15–6:15)
`[V: Pull back — the crowd from the opening, but now warm threads are lighting up between people, one honest connection at a time.]`
If you feel lonely, you are not defective, and you are not the only one. You're a social animal, running ancient software, in a world it wasn't built for. The feeling is real, it's valid, and it's pointing you somewhere: toward each other.

`[V: The little bird, no longer dim, sits with one friend under a warm light.]`
We are a species that survived by holding on to one another. You still carry that. And the cure for the oldest loneliness is also the simplest: reach out first. Someone out there is hoping you will.

### OUTRO (6:15–6:45)
`[V: End screen, two birds side by side, video cards float up.]`
If you needed to hear this today — you're not alone in that, either. Send this to someone you've been meaning to talk to. Sources below. See you in the next one.
