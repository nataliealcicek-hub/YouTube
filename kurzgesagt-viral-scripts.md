# Kurzgesagt-Style Viral Scripts — A Pack of 5

Built directly on the channel analysis and the 6 viral archetypes in this repo
(`kurzgesagt-viral-video-analysis.md`). Each script is engineered to stack the
viral DNA: **topic-as-spectacle · second person, present tense · scale whiplash ·
concrete metaphor · one high-arousal emotion · curiosity-gap title · optimistic
landing · language-agnostic visuals.**

**Format key:** `[VISUAL]` = animation direction · **NARRATION** = Steve-Taylor-style VO · `(MUSIC)` = Epic-Mountain-style cue.

Each script is written to run ~5–7 min (~900–1,200 words). The earlier repo script
*"You Have a Second Brain"* is the long-form (~10 min) example.

## SCRIPT 1 — "What If You Fell Into the Deepest Hole on Earth?"
**Archetype A (Cosmic/physical "what if you…") · Emotion: wonder + dread · Highest hit-rate pattern**

### HOOK (0:00–0:40)
`[VISUAL: A tiny cartoon human stands on a flat green field, toes at the edge of a perfectly black circle. Camera slowly tilts down into infinite dark.]`

**NARRATION:**
Imagine we dug a hole. Not a big hole — *the* hole. Straight down, through the crust, through the mantle, all the way to the other side of the planet. And now imagine you jump in.

`[VISUAL: The human peers over the edge, gulps, and steps off. Title slams in as they fall into black.]`

**NARRATION:**
What happens next is one of the strangest journeys a human body could ever take. So let's take it — one second at a time.

`(MUSIC: slow, curious descending motif.)`
`[VISUAL: TITLE — "What If You Fell Through the Earth?"]`

### BEAT 1 — The Fall (0:40–2:00)
`[VISUAL: Speed lines. A speedometer ticks up.]`
**NARRATION:** For the first few seconds it feels like any fall. But there's no bottom coming. You keep accelerating — 50 miles an hour, then 100, then faster than a race car — as gravity pulls you toward the center of the world.
`[VISUAL: The falling human passes glowing rock layers, each labeled: crust, mantle. Temperature gauge climbs alarmingly.]`
**NARRATION:** Of course, in reality, you'd have some problems. The heat down here can top 5,000 degrees — hotter than the surface of some stars. The pressure would crush you. So for this journey, let's give you an impossible, indestructible suit. Now — keep falling.

### BEAT 2 — The Twist at the Center (2:00–3:30)
`(MUSIC: tension builds, then a sudden hush.)`
`[VISUAL: The human rockets toward a glowing point at the exact center of the Earth.]`
**NARRATION:** After about 21 minutes, you reach the center. And here, something bizarre happens. At the very core, the entire planet is pulling on you *equally, in every direction at once.*
`[VISUAL: Arrows point at the human from all sides. They hang, weightless, spinning gently.]`
**NARRATION:** For one strange moment, you weigh nothing. You are, briefly, floating at the heart of the world. But you're not stopping. You're moving too fast. So you shoot straight *past* the center — and now, for the first time, gravity is pulling you *backward.* You're falling *up.*

### BEAT 3 — The Other Side (3:30–4:40)
`[VISUAL: The human decelerates, rising toward a pinprick of light on the far side of the planet.]`
**NARRATION:** You slow, and slow, and slow — until you arrive at the surface on the opposite side of the Earth, and for a split second, your face pops out of the hole. If someone were standing there, you could say hello.
`[VISUAL: A surprised llama on the far side watches a human head briefly emerge, then fall back down.]`
**NARRATION:** But you don't grab the edge. So you fall back in. And you'd do this forever — back and forth, side to side, roughly every 42 minutes — a human pendulum swinging through the center of the world.

### PAYOFF (4:40–6:00)
`(MUSIC: the wide, hopeful "wonder" theme.)`
`[VISUAL: Pull back — the whole Earth, tiny human oscillating through it like a heartbeat.]`
**NARRATION:** You'll never fall through the Earth. The hole is impossible, the heat is lethal, and the planet would never let you. But the fact that we can *know* exactly what would happen — the speed, the weightless center, the 42-minute rhythm — using nothing but physics and imagination, is its own kind of magic.
`[VISUAL: The human, back on the surface, looks down at the ground beneath their feet with new eyes.]`
**NARRATION:** You are standing on top of the strangest object you'll ever meet. And most of us walk across it every day without ever looking down. Maybe today, you will.

`[VISUAL: Kurzgesagt bird waves, dives into a tiny hole. End screen.]`

**VIRAL NOTES:** Title is a pure curiosity gap. Hook is 2nd-person + impossible scenario your brain auto-simulates. Weightless-center = the "whoa" scale moment. Language-agnostic (physics + visuals). Lands on wonder + agency.

## SCRIPT 2 — "There Is a Number So Big It Could Break Your Brain"
**Archetype E (Pure scale/perspective) · Emotion: vertigo + awe · Hypnotic, rewatchable, travels globally**

### HOOK (0:00–0:35)
`[VISUAL: A single grain of sand on a fingertip.]`
**NARRATION:** Here is a grain of sand. Now here is every grain of sand on every beach on Earth.
`[VISUAL: Zoom out explosively — fingertip → beach → all beaches → a staggering pile.]`
**NARRATION:** It's a number so large you can't picture it. And yet, by the end of this video, that number is going to feel *tiny.* Almost nothing. Let me show you how.
`(MUSIC: minimal, ticking, building.)`
`[VISUAL: TITLE — "How Big Can Numbers Get?"]`

### BEAT 1 — Everyday Big (0:35–1:45)
`[VISUAL: A counter ticking through everyday large numbers.]`
**NARRATION:** A thousand seconds is about 17 minutes. A million seconds is 11 days. But a *billion* seconds? That's over 31 years. The jump from million to billion is so huge that if you'd started counting at the fall of Rome, you still wouldn't be done.
`[VISUAL: A tired cartoon counter, unshaven, still counting since ancient Rome.]`

### BEAT 2 — Cosmic Big (1:45–3:15)
`[VISUAL: Zoom through stars, galaxies.]`
**NARRATION:** Now let's go cosmic. There are roughly 100 billion stars in our galaxy, and maybe two trillion galaxies in the observable universe. Multiply it all out, and the number of atoms in the entire universe is about a 1 followed by 80 zeros.
`[VISUAL: An 80-digit number sprawls across the screen, dwarfing a tiny human.]`
**NARRATION:** That is, as far as physical reality goes, close to the biggest number that actually *counts* anything real. And here's the twist — in mathematics, it's a rounding error. Barely a beginning.

### BEAT 3 — The Twist: Beyond Reality (3:15–4:45)
`(MUSIC: the "reveal" opens up, awe-struck.)`
`[VISUAL: The 80-digit number shrinks to a dot as bigger numbers loom.]`
**NARRATION:** A googol is a 1 with a *hundred* zeros — already more than the atoms in the universe. A googolplex is a 1 followed by a googol of zeros. If you tried to physically write it out, one zero per atom, you would run out of universe long, long before you finished.
`[VISUAL: The universe fills entirely with zeros and still isn't enough.]`
**NARRATION:** These numbers are real. They're perfectly valid. They just describe quantities that reality itself is too *small* to contain.

### PAYOFF (4:45–6:00)
`[VISUAL: Pull back to the single grain of sand from the opening.]`
**NARRATION:** Remember all the sand on Earth? Against a googolplex, it isn't even a rounding error. It's nothing. And yet here's the beautiful part: your mind — this three-pound object inside your skull — can hold a number that the entire universe cannot physically write down.
`[VISUAL: A tiny human holds a glowing googolplex in cupped hands, dwarfing the cosmos behind them.]`
**NARRATION:** The universe may be unimaginably big. But imagination might be the one thing bigger.

`[VISUAL: Kurzgesagt bird holds up a "1" with too many zeros to fit. End screen.]`

**VIRAL NOTES:** Relentless zoom = hypnotic + rewatchable. Almost no culture-specific reference → dubs and travels. Payoff flips "we're tiny" into "our minds are huge" = shareable optimism.

## SCRIPT 3 — "Right Now, a War Is Being Fought Inside You"
**Archetype C (Your body is an epic / body horror) · Emotion: "that's happening in ME?!" · Maximally personal**

### HOOK (0:00–0:40)
`[VISUAL: Peaceful shot of a person sleeping. Slow push in through the skin.]`
**NARRATION:** You feel fine. You're calm, safe, maybe a little bored watching this. But right now, inside your body, thousands of soldiers are dying to keep you that way.
`[VISUAL: Reveal a microscopic battlefield inside a blood vessel — immune cells vs. invaders, chaos.]`
**NARRATION:** Every single day, your body fights a war you never notice — and today, you're going to watch a single battle from the front lines.
`(MUSIC: tense, cinematic, military drums under strings.)`
`[VISUAL: TITLE — "The War Inside Your Body"]`

### BEAT 1 — The Breach (0:40–2:00)
`[VISUAL: A splinter pierces cartoon skin. Bacteria pour through the gap like invaders storming a wall.]`
**NARRATION:** It starts with something tiny. A splinter. A scratch. A gap in your defenses barely wider than a hair. And through it, an army pours in — bacteria, replicating every 20 minutes, doubling, and doubling again.
`[VISUAL: The bacteria multiply exponentially, a spreading red tide.]`
**NARRATION:** Left alone, a handful becomes millions in hours. This is a race. And your body has just noticed.

### BEAT 2 — The First Responders (2:00–3:30)
`[VISUAL: Blob-like immune cells (macrophages) charge in and start eating invaders whole.]`
**NARRATION:** First to arrive are your macrophages — living creatures that fight by *eating.* They engulf the invaders whole, swallowing dozens each. But they're outnumbered. So they do something clever: they scream. Chemically. Calling for backup.
`[VISUAL: A macrophage releases glowing distress signals; the area swells red and hot.]`
**NARRATION:** That redness, that heat, that swelling around a wound? That's not the infection. That's your army *arriving.*

### BEAT 3 — The Twist: The Kill Order (3:30–4:50)
`(MUSIC: escalates to a dramatic peak.)`
`[VISUAL: A dendritic cell grabs a piece of the enemy and races through a tunnel toward a command center — the lymph node.]`
**NARRATION:** Meanwhile, a messenger grabs a piece of the enemy and sprints to your lymph nodes — the war room — where it shows the sample to your most powerful weapons: your T-cells. And here's the terrifying, beautiful part. Somewhere in your body is a T-cell shaped *precisely* to destroy this exact invader — one in millions. Your body finds it. And gives the order to clone it, thousands of times.
`[VISUAL: An army of identical elite cells mass-produced, then marching to the wound.]`
**NARRATION:** An elite force, custom-built for *this* enemy, that did not exist an hour ago.

### PAYOFF (4:50–6:10)
`(MUSIC: resolves — triumphant, then warm.)`
`[VISUAL: The battlefield quiets. The wound closes. The person sleeps on, undisturbed.]`
**NARRATION:** The invaders are wiped out. The wound heals. And you never knew a thing. But your body did something even more remarkable — it *remembered.* A few of those custom soldiers stay behind, on guard, sometimes for the rest of your life. So that next time, the war is over before it begins.
`[VISUAL: A single memory cell stands sentinel in a calm vessel, watching.]`
**NARRATION:** You are not a fragile thing. You are a fortress, defended by billions of lives working in silence so that you can sit here, safe, and never have to know. The least we can do is be a little amazed by them.

`[VISUAL: Kurzgesagt bird in tiny armor salutes. End screen.]`

**VIRAL NOTES:** Reframes the viewer's own body as an action movie → maximally personal. Fear + awe = high arousal. "That's happening in me right now" is the share trigger. Lands on gratitude/awe, not fear.

## SCRIPT 4 — "If the Universe Is So Big, Where Is Everyone?"
**Archetype B (Existential mystery) · Emotion: unsettled fascination · People share it to DEBATE**

### HOOK (0:00–0:45)
`[VISUAL: A lone human on a hill at night, looking up at a sky drowning in stars.]`
**NARRATION:** The universe is unimaginably old, and staggeringly huge. Hundreds of billions of stars in our galaxy alone. Billions of galaxies beyond it. The numbers are so overwhelming that intelligent life shouldn't just be *possible* — it should be *everywhere.*
`[VISUAL: Zoom out through galaxies teeming with possibility. Then — silence. Empty. Dark.]`
**NARRATION:** And yet, when we look and listen, we find… nothing. No signals. No ships. No neighbors. Just silence. So where is everybody?
`(MUSIC: vast, lonely, slow.)`
`[VISUAL: TITLE — "The Fermi Paradox — Where Is Everyone?"]`

### BEAT 1 — It Should Be Crowded (0:45–2:00)
`[VISUAL: A galaxy map filling with green dots — potential civilizations.]`
**NARRATION:** Do the math and it gets stranger. Even if a single civilization got the tiniest head start and spread slowly between the stars, it could colonize an entire galaxy in a few million years — a blink, in cosmic time. Our galaxy is old enough that this should have happened many times over. The sky should be busy. It isn't. This contradiction has a name: the Fermi Paradox.

### BEAT 2 — The Great Filter (2:00–3:40)
`(MUSIC: a cold, uneasy shift.)`
`[VISUAL: A long staircase from "dead chemistry" up to "galactic civilization." A dark barrier blocks it somewhere.]`
**NARRATION:** Here is the unsettling answer some scientists propose: maybe something *stops* life. A wall. A Great Filter. A step so hard that almost nothing makes it past. The question that should keep you up at night is — *where* is the filter? Is it behind us, or ahead of us?
`[VISUAL: The barrier flickers between two positions on the staircase — below humanity, or just above it.]`
**NARRATION:** If the filter is *behind* us — if life, or intelligence, is the near-impossible step — then we may be alone, precious, and safe. But if the filter is *ahead* of us — a challenge no civilization survives — then the silence isn't peaceful. It's a warning.

### BEAT 3 — The Twist: The Quiet Reasons (3:40–5:00)
`[VISUAL: A gallery of possibilities as illustrated cards flip past.]`
**NARRATION:** Or maybe the answer is gentler. Maybe they're out there, but too far, and the universe too vast, for any signal to ever reach us. Maybe they're quiet on purpose. Maybe we're simply early — one of the very first, arriving before the galactic party has begun.
`[VISUAL: A tiny Earth at a party venue that's still being set up, no other guests yet.]`
**NARRATION:** We don't know which of these is true. And that's exactly what makes it one of the most important questions we can ask.

### PAYOFF (5:00–6:15)
`(MUSIC: turns from dread to quiet resolve.)`
`[VISUAL: Back to the human on the hill, still looking up.]`
**NARRATION:** Every answer to the Fermi Paradox carries a message. If we're alone, then Earth is the most precious thing in existence, and it's ours to protect. If we're early, then the future of the galaxy might be written by *us* — and the choices we make now, whether we get past our own filters, could echo for a billion years.
`[VISUAL: The human, small under the stars, but standing a little taller.]`
**NARRATION:** The silence is frightening. But it might also mean the story of intelligent life in the universe is just getting started — and that, for now, we're the ones holding the pen.

`[VISUAL: Kurzgesagt bird points a tiny telescope at the empty sky. End screen.]`

**VIRAL NOTES:** Unresolved question → comment-section debate → shares. "Filter behind or ahead of us" is the chilling, quotable hook. Every ending gives the viewer a *stake*, which drives "what do YOU think?" engagement.

## SCRIPT 5 — "You Will Die. Here's Why That's Not the Whole Story."
**Archetype F (Emotional/philosophical departure) · Emotion: catharsis / chills · The rarest, most-shared "this changed me" type**

### HOOK (0:00–0:50)
`[VISUAL: A single candle burning in the dark. No narration for 3 seconds. Just the flame.]`
**NARRATION:** One day, you will die. Everyone you love will die. The sun will die. And eventually, the universe itself will grow cold and dark and empty, and nothing you did will be remembered by anyone.
`[VISUAL: The candle gutters. The screen goes fully black for a beat.]`
**NARRATION:** That's the scary version. It's also, strangely, not the version that has to make you sad. Let me show you the other way to look at it.
`(MUSIC: soft, warm piano begins in the dark.)`
`[VISUAL: TITLE — "Optimistic Nihilism" over a slow sunrise.]`

### BEAT 1 — The Cold Truth (0:50–2:10)
`[VISUAL: Deep-time zoom — Earth aging, the sun swelling, stars winking out over eons.]`
**NARRATION:** On a long enough timeline, nothing lasts. Not empires, not monuments, not this planet, not the stars. From the universe's point of view, your entire life — every joy, every heartbreak, every ordinary Tuesday — is a flicker too brief to measure. It can feel like a crushing weight. If nothing matters in the end, why do anything at all?

### BEAT 2 — The Turn (2:10–3:40)
`(MUSIC: the piano is joined by rising strings — the pivot.)`
`[VISUAL: The camera turns away from the cold future and back toward a single, ordinary, sunlit human moment — two friends laughing.]`
**NARRATION:** But look at it from the other side. If nothing we do matters in some grand, cosmic ledger — then the only thing that *can* matter is what we do right now. There's no script. No one is grading you. The universe isn't watching and shaking its head. You are free.
`[VISUAL: The human, once crushed by the weight, straightens up — the weight lifts and floats away.]`
**NARRATION:** Free to decide what has meaning. Free to build it, chase it, love it, right here, in the only moment that was ever real: this one.

### BEAT 3 — The Odds of You (3:40–5:00)
`[VISUAL: A dizzying cascade of "what had to happen for you to exist" — stars forming, life beginning, an unbroken chain of ancestors.]`
**NARRATION:** And consider the odds. For *you* to exist, an unbroken chain of life stretching back four billion years had to survive every ice age, every predator, every disaster — and then two specific people had to meet, out of billions. The chance of you being here, being *you*, is so small it's effectively zero. And yet — here you are.
`[VISUAL: A single, glowing "you" standing at the end of an infinite chain of light.]`
**NARRATION:** You are the universe, briefly, waking up and looking at itself. That has never happened before, and it will never happen again.

### PAYOFF (5:00–6:20)
`(MUSIC: full, warm, resolving.)`
`[VISUAL: The candle from the opening, relit, next to a window with morning light.]`
**NARRATION:** So yes — one day it all ends. But that's not a reason to give up. It's the best reason there could be to *start.* To call the person. To make the thing. To go outside. To be kind, not because a god or a universe demands it, but because *you* choose to, in your brief, impossible, unrepeatable flicker of existence.
`[VISUAL: The small human steps out the door into a bright, ordinary, beautiful day.]`
**NARRATION:** Nothing we do will matter forever. Which means everything we do can matter completely, right now. And if that's true — then you have some living to do. So go. We'll see you next time.

`[VISUAL: Kurzgesagt bird sits on a hill watching the sunrise, content. End screen.]`

**VIRAL NOTES:** Drops "explainer" mode for pure meaning → shared as "this changed how I think" (highest-value share). Opens on the channel's core thesis. Turns dread into agency — the exact emotional arc that made *Optimistic Nihilism* and *The Egg* huge.

## Quick reference — how the pack covers the archetypes

| Script | Archetype | Core emotion | Best-for |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 · Fall Through the Earth | A — Cosmic what-if | Wonder + dread | Broadest reach, safest bet |
| 2 · How Big Can Numbers Get | E — Pure scale | Vertigo + awe | Global/dub-friendly, rewatchable |
| 3 · The War Inside Your Body | C — Body epic | "In ME right now?!" | Most personal, high retention |
| 4 · The Fermi Paradox | B — Existential mystery | Unsettled fascination | Max comments/debate |
| 5 · Optimistic Nihilism | F — Emotional departure | Catharsis / chills | Highest-value "this changed me" shares |

**Recommended first upload:** Script 1 (Archetype A has the highest historical hit-rate and lowest barrier to entry). Save Script 5 for when the channel has an emotionally invested audience — it rewards existing loyalty the most.
