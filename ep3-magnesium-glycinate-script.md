## Pharmacology / physiology the animation is built on

| Claim in narration | Basis |
|---|---|
| ~25 g total body magnesium; ~60% in bone, ~27% in muscle, <1% in serum | standard distribution figures; serum is what is measured but reflects little of total stores |
| Cofactor for 300+ enzymatic reactions | widely cited enzymology figure (some sources say 600+) |
| ATP is biologically active as the Mg-ATP complex | ATP must be chelated to Mg²⁺ to serve as substrate for kinases and ATPases |
| Magnesium oxide is poorly soluble and poorly absorbed | bioavailability commonly reported around 4%, far below organic/chelated forms |
| Unabsorbed magnesium draws water into the bowel osmotically | the basis of magnesium's laxative effect (milk of magnesia) |
| Magnesium bisglycinate = one Mg²⁺ chelated by two glycine molecules, neutral and stable | amino acid chelate chemistry; the two glycines form stable ring structures around the ion |
| Absorption partly via amino-acid/peptide routes rather than the saturable mineral channel | **proposed** mechanism for improved tolerability; supported but not settled — narration says "appears to" |
| Mineral absorption uses saturable TRPM6/TRPM7 transcellular transport plus paracellular passive flow | established magnesium absorption physiology in the small intestine |
| In muscle, Ca²⁺ triggers contraction; Mg²⁺ powers the pumps that withdraw it, permitting relaxation | Mg is required by SERCA and Na⁺/K⁺-ATPase; Mg and Ca act as physiological counterweights |
| Mg²⁺ sits in the NMDA receptor pore as a voltage-dependent block | classic NMDA receptor Mg²⁺ block; relieved by depolarisation; low Mg raises excitability |
| Glycine is an inhibitory neurotransmitter opening chloride channels | glycine receptor (GlyR) is a ligand-gated chloride channel |
| Glycine lowers core body temperature before sleep | documented effect via peripheral vasodilation; associated with improved sleep quality |

## The 12 blocks

| # | Narration (VO) | Visual |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | Every time your heart beats, a muscle relaxes, or a nerve goes quiet, one mineral has to be there. And most people are running slightly short of it. | Figure in the dark; heart pulse, a muscle, a nerve firing; sparse teal specks, too few. |
| 2 | Magnesium. You carry about twenty-five grams. Sixty percent is locked inside your bones. Less than one percent floats in your blood, which is where we measure it. | Figure fills with proportional teal: bone reservoir vs muscle vs a thin blood line. |
| 3 | It's a cofactor for over three hundred enzymes. And almost every molecule of ATP, the fuel your cells run on, only works while magnesium is bound to it. | ATP molecule rotating; a teal ion clicks into it; gold energy releases; kinases fire. |
| 4 | But swallowing magnesium is harder than it sounds. Cheap forms like magnesium oxide barely dissolve, so only a small fraction ever crosses the gut wall. | Gut lumen: chalky undissolved oxide chunks; few ions cross the villi wall. |
| 5 | And what stays behind pulls water into your intestine by osmosis. That is why a large dose of the wrong magnesium sends you straight to the bathroom. | Water molecules dragged into the lumen; the bowel distends; acid-yellow distress ripple. |
| 6 | Magnesium glycinate is built differently. One magnesium ion is clamped between two molecules of glycine, an amino acid, forming a neutral, stable chelate. | Two lavender glycines close around a teal ion; charge neutralises; the chelate locks. |
| 7 | And that wrapper changes how it travels. Rather than queueing for the saturable mineral channels, it appears to ride amino-acid absorption routes instead. | Wall cross-section: a congested TRPM6 mineral channel vs a free-flowing amino-acid transporter. |
| 8 | Once through, the package comes apart. Magnesium moves out to muscle and bone, and the glycine is released free. Two active compounds, delivered as one. | In the blood, the chelate splits; teal travels one way, lavender the other. |
| 9 | In muscle, calcium is the signal to contract. Magnesium is the counterweight — powering the pumps that haul calcium back out so the fibre can finally release. | Sarcomere: amber calcium floods and shortens it; teal-powered pumps withdraw calcium; it lengthens. |
| 10 | In the brain, magnesium sits inside the NMDA receptor like a plug, blocking the channel until a signal is strong enough. Run low, and neurons fire too easily. | Synapse: teal ion lodged in the NMDA pore; with it gone, acid-yellow overfiring. |
| 11 | Glycine, meanwhile, is an inhibitory neurotransmitter in its own right. It opens chloride channels that quiet neurons, and it lowers your core temperature before sleep. | Glycine receptor opens; chloride floods in; neuron dims; a body-temperature curve falls. |
| 12 | So the calm isn't one effect. It's two, arriving together — a mineral that lets your cells rest, carried by a molecule that does the very same thing. | Pull back to the figure; teal and lavender settle through it in a slow shared pulse. |
